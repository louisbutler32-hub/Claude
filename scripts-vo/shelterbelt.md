# America Tried To Stop The Weather

**Format** vertical short · **Runtime** ~52s · ~120 words · Voice: flat,
declarative, one fact per line. The facts are absurd enough on their own.

---

## Script

```
0:00   America once tried to stop the weather.
       With trees.

0:05   1934. The Great Plains are blowing away.

0:09   The drought kills the grass,
       and nothing is holding the soil down any more.

0:15   Dust storms reach New York.

0:19   So Roosevelt orders a wall.

0:23   Not concrete. Trees.

0:26   A belt a hundred miles wide,
       from Canada to Texas.

0:32   Two hundred and twenty million of them.

0:37   Planted by hand, in rows, across six states.

0:42   Critics called it the most expensive way
       to waste a forest.

0:47   The wind slowed. The soil stayed.

0:50   Most of it is still standing.

0:53   China started copying it in 1978.
       They are still planting.
```

## Facts

- **1934**: FDR launched the Shelterbelt Project (formally the Prairie
  States Forestry Project, planting 1935–42). Solid.
- **Dust to the east coast**: the May 1934 storm carried Plains soil over
  Chicago, New York and Washington. Solid.
- **~220 million trees** across six states — North Dakota to Texas — in a
  zone about 100 miles wide. Standard figures.
- **Critics**: the project was widely mocked at the time, and Forest Service
  scientists themselves doubted trees would survive the Plains. Fair to say
  critics called it a waste; do not put a specific quote in anyone's mouth.
- **It worked**: wind erosion dropped measurably in sheltered fields. Many
  belts were later torn out for larger machinery, so "most of it is still
  standing" is the honest ceiling — do not claim all of it.
- **China**: the Three-North Shelterbelt began in 1978 and is still being
  planted, scheduled to run to 2050. "Still planting" is accurate; avoid
  quoting a length, the published figures vary wildly.
