# The Richest Man in History

**Format** vertical short · **Runtime** 33s · ~85 words · Voice: plain
storybook. Short declarative lines, present tense, one fact each. Read it
flat — the facts do the work.

---

## Script — read straight down

```
0:00   This man went on holiday and crashed an economy for twelve years.

0:04   This is Mansa Musa.
       Nobody can count how rich he is.

0:08   In 1324, he walks to Mecca.

0:11   He brings sixty thousand people.
       And camels carrying gold.

0:15   In Cairo, he starts giving it away.
       To anyone who asks.

0:20   So much gold that gold stops being worth anything.

0:24   It takes Cairo twelve years to recover.

0:27   Fifty years later, a mapmaker in Spain
       draws him on the map of the world.

0:32   Holding a lump of gold.
```

**Delivery:** flat and unhurried, but don't leave air between lines — the
camera is moving fast underneath you. The only pause that matters is before "Holding
a lump of gold."

---

## Map

No jump cuts. The camera holds, then **zooms the whole distance in about
two thirds of a second** and settles — one move per line, with each prop
popping in on the word.

| Time | Camera | On the map |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Mali, raked hard | Gold floods West Africa |
| 0:04 | **fast zoom down** onto Niani | City marker pops |
| 0:08 | **fast zoom out** | Mecca drops in on the far side — the distance is the joke |
| 0:11 | quick reframe east, turning | Caravan arrow leaves Niani |
| 0:15 | **fast zoom** onto Cairo | Arrow lands; first coins pop |
| 0:20 | slow push only | Coins keep landing, one per beat, past the point of looking tidy |
| 0:24 | hold | The pile sits there |
| 0:27 | **fast pull** all the way out to the known world | Catalan Atlas panel pops in beside Mali |
| 0:32 | slow drift | Hold on the medieval map |

## Facts, and how far they can be pushed

- **"Nobody can count how rich he is"** is the honest phrasing. The
  $400-billion figures online are invented — there is no workable way to
  price a 14th-century gold economy. Do not quote a number.
- **1324** hajj: solid.
- **Sixty thousand people, camels of gold**: from medieval Arabic accounts
  (al-Umari, later Ibn Khaldun), not records. Say "he brings sixty thousand
  people", never "records show".
- **The Cairo gold collapse and the twelve years** is al-Umari, who visited
  about twelve years later and wrote that the price still had not recovered.
  One man's account, and a real one.
- **The Catalan Atlas, 1375**: solid, and public domain — it is the closing
  image, `public/assets/props/catalan-atlas-mansa-musa.jpg`.
