## Facts, and how far they can be pushed

- **"Never beat them in the field"** — true of the field armies: Kalka
  (1223), Chmielnik, Legnica and Mohi were all Mongol victories. Several
  *fortified* places did hold: Esztergom's citadel, Székesfehérvár, Trogir.
  Say "in the open field", not "never won anything".
- **Kyiv** fell 6 December 1240. The two hundred houses is Giovanni da Pian
  del Carpine, riding through in 1246 — a real eyewitness line, not legend.
- **Legnica 9 April, Mohi 11 April 1241** — two days apart, roughly 400
  miles apart. This is the single best fact in the video; do not bury it.
- **Wiener Neustadt**, not Vienna. Raiding parties, not a siege.
- **Ogedei died 11 December 1241**; Batu withdrew in spring 1242. The
  succession-crisis explanation is the traditional one and is contested.
- **The weather** is Büntgen & Di Cosmo (2016, *Scientific Reports*): an
  unusually cold, wet early 1242 turned the Hungarian plain marshy and cut
  the pasture the Mongol horse herds depended on. Present it as the rival
  theory it is — "the tree rings tell a different story" — not as settled.
- Hungary's losses are estimated at 15-25% of the population, higher on the
  plain. If you quote a number, quote a range.
