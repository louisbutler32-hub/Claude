# Geo shorts

Sixty-second vertical explainers on a satellite map, in the format of the
map-fact channels: one continuous camera over real satellite imagery,
countries wearing their flags, a number every few seconds in an engraved
callout, a flat cut-away diagram when the map can't show the point, and the
narration a phrase at a time in small white type at 80% of the frame.

```
src/geo/
  GeoCanvas.tsx     the map: camera, satellite tiles, colour grade, context
  Basemap.tsx       the tile layer — which tiles exist, where they sit
  projection.ts     flat Mercator camera (shares keys/easing with src/maps)
  shapes.ts         countries and states → screen paths, antimeridian-safe
  layers.tsx        Highlight, Wash, Label, Measure, Route, Dashed, Pin, Rings, Sign, Spot
  hud.tsx           Callout, BigNumber, Slam, IconRow, Tag, Captions, Overlay
  diagrams.tsx      StraitSection — the cut-away with depth marks, ice, a comparison
  flags.tsx         flags drawn in code (usa, russia, france, spain, haiti, panama, …)
  icons.tsx         the pictograms for callouts and reasons rows
  timing.ts         narration → beats: every visual is keyed off a line's start
  fonts.ts          Montserrat / Roboto / Cinzel, loaded from public/fonts
  Thumbnails.tsx    9:16 stills for the channel grid
  data/regions.json where the deep tile levels are built (and read)
  data/shapes.json  generated by scripts/build-geo-data.mjs — do not hand-edit
  bering/           Why is there no bridge between Russia and Alaska?
  louisiana/        The Louisiana Purchase, for 3 cents an acre
  darien/           The Darién Gap, the hole in the longest road on Earth
```

Each short is one file — `bering/BeringShort.tsx` — plus its `timing.json`
(written by the voice build) and its `upload.md`.

## The style, as measured

Both reference videos were pulled apart frame by frame before anything was
built. What the format actually is:

- **One camera, no cuts.** Sixty seconds of continuous zooms and pans over
  a satellite map; the only hard transitions are into and out of a diagram.
- **Flags on the ground.** The country under discussion wears its flag,
  stretched over its outline, with a rippling cloth texture and a white
  halo that breathes. Everything else stays satellite.
- **A number every ~8 s**, in an engraved serif with a gold glow and an
  icon — "7.2 MILLION $", "900 TONS", "× 288". Years are big geometric bold.
- **Captions**: ~46 px Roboto-weight sans, sentence case, white with a soft
  dark shadow, centred at 80% of the frame height, a phrase of two to five
  words at a time, changing with the voice and never animated.
- **A cut-away when the map can't say it**: pale sky, navy cliffs, a blue
  water gradient, a dashed depth line, orange country names. The comparison
  slides in beside it with a yellow seam.
- **The hook is a question or a "not many people know"**, the payoff is the
  biggest number, and there is no call to action.

## The ground

**Satellite imagery** is NASA's Blue Marble Next Generation (August 2004,
with topography and bathymetry). NASA imagery is public domain — safe to
monetise, nothing to credit. `scripts/build-geo-basemap.py` downloads it
into `.geo/` and cuts it into 512 px Web Mercator tiles under
`public/assets/geo/tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}.jpg`: levels 0–5 for the whole planet,
deeper levels only inside the regions in `data/regions.json`. Both the
download and the tiles are gitignored (a few hundred MB, and derived).

```bash
pip install pillow numpy
npm run geo:tiles            # ~10 minutes the first time; incremental after
```

Blue Marble is ~460 m per pixel at best, so the map is sharp down to a frame
about 300 km wide and soft below that. A flat sea colour fades in over the
imagery at the deepest zooms so the ocean floor doesn't blow up into
blotches; land at those zooms is carried by the flag highlights.

**Outlines** are Natural Earth 10m (public domain), extracted and simplified
by `scripts/build-geo-data.mjs` into `data/shapes.json`. Add a country or
state to `WANT` there and rebuild:

```bash
npm run geo:data
```

Rings that cross the antimeridian are stored unwrapped past +180°, and the
renderer draws every polygon at whichever ±360° copy is nearest the camera,
so a camera parked over the Bering Strait sees both shores.

## Building a short

1. **Script** — `scripts-vo/geo-<name>.json`. One line per beat, each with a
   `scene` name, the `text`, optional `say` (respellings for the voice) and
   `cap` (the caption chunks, two to five words each). Aim for ~160 words:
   the voice reads about 175 wpm and the gaps get squeezed to fit 60 s.
2. **Voice** — `python3 scripts/make-vo.py geo-<name>` writes
   `public/assets/vo/geo-<name>.mp3` and `src/geo/<name>/timing.json`.
   Kokoro (Apache-2.0, runs locally) — the same voice as the essays.
3. **Scenes** — copy `bering/BeringShort.tsx`. Every beat is
   `B.<scene>.start + offset`, so a regenerated voice re-times the edit.
   Camera keys are `{ at, lon, lat, scale }`; `scale` is how many pixels the
   whole world would be (3,000 continental, 70,000 a strait, 500,000 two
   islands).
4. **Regions** — if the camera goes below a frame ~2,000 km wide somewhere
   new, add that box to `data/regions.json` and rerun `npm run geo:tiles`.
5. **Render** — register in `src/Root.tsx`, add the npm scripts, then
   `npm run geo:<name>`, `npm run geo:<name>:thumb`, and write `upload.md`.

The music bed is synthesised by `scripts/make-geo-bed.py` (sine waves, so
nothing to license) and sits ~14 dB under the voice. A short can carry a
licensed track instead: `scripts/make-geo-music.py <name> <track.mp3>` cuts
a levelled 60 s bed into `public/audio/geo-<name>-music.mp3` (gitignored —
the licence is the channel's, not the repo's) and the short takes it as a
`music` prop, so the default render has no bed and `npm run geo:<name>:music`
adds it. The Darién short is wired this way, with Kevin MacLeod's
"Accralate" (CC BY 4.0, credited in its `upload.md`).

## Accuracy notes

Hand-drawn rings — the Louisiana Purchase, the Atrato floodplain, the
Darién park — are approximate and say so in their descriptions. Every
number spoken is sourced in the short's `upload.md`.
