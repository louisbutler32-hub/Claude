## Description

What if Napoleon had won at Waterloo? In February 1815 he escaped from Elba with about a thousand men, landed at Golfe-Juan on 1 March and entered Paris on 20 March with the army behind him. On 18 June at Waterloo, roughly 73,000 French soldiers met 68,000 under Wellington; it was close, until 50,000 Prussians under Blücher arrived. But a French victory would have changed almost nothing. Waterloo was one battle on one front: around 850,000 allied soldiers were converging on France from Austria, Russia, Prussia, Britain and Italy, against about 250,000 Frenchmen. Outnumbered more than three to one, Napoleon would simply have lost the next battle. He abdicated on 22 June and died on Saint Helena in 1821.

Every frame is a real satellite map (NASA Blue Marble) drawn in code. The arrows show the direction of the allied armies, not exact routes.
