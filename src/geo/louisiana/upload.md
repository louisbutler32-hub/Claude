## Description

Not many Americans know that their country nearly doubled in size overnight, for less than three cents an acre. In 1803 Louisiana was a French territory stretching from the Gulf of Mexico to Canada — 828,000 square miles, over 2.1 million km². President Jefferson only wanted New Orleans, the port that controlled the Mississippi, and was ready to pay up to $10 million for it. But Napoleon was losing Haiti to a slave revolt and a war with Britain was coming, so he offered all of it: the whole of Louisiana for $15 million — about $380 million in today's money, roughly $18 per square mile. The land now covers all or part of 15 states, from Louisiana to Montana. An acre of Iowa farmland now sells for over $11,000 — about 400,000 times what Jefferson paid.
