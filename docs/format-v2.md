# Format v2 — the reboot

Derived from the videos that actually win this niche, not from taste.

## The benchmark

**The Paint Explainer — "What Dying on Every Planet Would Be Like"**
12,556,363 views · 8:16 · hand-drawn.

Same topic, same length, same art style as our Planets video, which got
2,400. So the gap is not the subject and not the medium. It is the
grammar.

Measured over two minutes of it: **28 distinct visual setups in 120
seconds — a new visual every 4.3 seconds** — and the picture keeps
moving inside each one.

## The seven rules

### 1. Second person, present, continuous
The viewer is the protagonist and never stops being it. The benchmark
says "you'd notice", "you'd feel", "you'd encounter" — hundreds of
times. It never says "a person would".

Ours said *"a lake exhaled and 1,746 people did not wake up."* Correct,
well written, and about somebody else. Rewrite: *"You are asleep three
hundred metres from the water."*

### 2. There is a character on screen, and things happen to him
The benchmark has an astronaut who waves his arms to show the air is
thick, shakes when he struggles, and whose suit **cracks and changes
colour from blue to yellow** as the acid takes it.

Our videos contain no people. That is the biggest single miss. A wonky
hand-drawn figure is on-brand for the channel and gives us the acting
layer we do not have.

### 3. Keep him alive on purpose
> "since that's a boring outcome let's assume you have a hypothetical
> suit that can block the radiation"

The benchmark openly cheats death so the descent can continue. That is
the retention engine: the viewer is always falling towards something
worse. Steal it outright.

### 4. Pay off inside every item, not at the end
Each planet in the benchmark is a descent: approach → clouds → surface →
deeper → core. The payoff is at the bottom of each one.

Ours climbed seven rungs to a ranking table at 7:33. That asks a viewer
who has no reason to trust the channel to invest seven and a half minutes
in advance. At 29 subscribers nobody pays that. **Drop the ranking
spine.**

### 5. A new visual every ~4 seconds, and it moves while the line runs
Not a static composition with a camera push over it. Things enter, move,
break, transform: a rocket arcs across, heat comes off in red wavy lines,
a dust storm sweeps horizontally, the suit cracks.

### 6. Text: a headline and number labels. Nothing else.
The benchmark has a big centred planet name, small labels next to
objects ("Size", "Gravity", "atmosphere"), and the numbers themselves
("92 bar", "450 degrees Celsius"). That is all.

**Kill:** chapter cards, captions that repeat the narration, the corner
progress chip. The benchmark has **no progress indicator of any kind**.

### 7. Hybrid art — real photographs for real things
The benchmark uses NASA-style photographic textures for the planets and
drawn art for the character, the icons and the diagrams. One real
photograph of sulfuric acid in a beaker appears as evidence.

So: **the drawing is the explaining language; the photograph is the
evidence.** The hand-drawn identity stays — it is the channel's name —
but anything that really exists and was really photographed gets shown.

## Sources for real images — verified reachable from here

| source | status | licence |
|---|---|---|
| NASA image API | HTTP 200, full-size assets download | public domain |
| Wikimedia Commons API | HTTP 200 **only with a descriptive user agent** (bare request → 429) | per-file metadata, checked per image |

Every fetched image records its licence and credit next to the file.
Nothing ships whose licence has not been read.

## What this does not change

Narration-as-master-clock, the exact-duration timing pass, procedural
audio, the contact-sheet review before render. Those work. Keep them.
